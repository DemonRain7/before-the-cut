# Before the Cut: playable prototype


## Playing rules version 14

Ask what's bothering the visitor. A question mark on the card and its test button shows the part they named. Each visit has two lamps. LIGHT tests eyes, GRIP tests hands, PORT tests the socket, PRESS tests temper. A new test costs one lamp. Open a finished test again for free. Compare its reading with the sheet and mark it yourself. Talk and listed questions are free. Two broken rows mean Trauma Team. One could be wear. At zero lamps a patient waits for 30 idle seconds. Only committed pushes use hidden patience. A first PRESS with a lamp reads TEMPER. The second push draws a personal warning. Confirming the third makes a steady visitor walk out unpaid or a drifting visitor lunge. You can still push with empty lamps. Only a funded reading earns TEMPER. Press SPACE for the fee plus 50. At closing a short silhouette film shows one admitted patient in the back room (a drifting one wakes and kills the crew), then the report gives the true rows and how many complaints guided the next test.

From the repository root, try `.\run-game.ps1 -Patient script -Language zh -TestNight K` and repeat with `-Patient mock`. K teaches the loop and contacts, and L includes silent and misleading patients. The local mock uses no paid calls. [Current pressure rules and measurements](../docs/development/play-loop-redesign/PROGRESS.md#hidden-patience)

## Reference for the November 21 version 12 build

The following development manual describes the earlier build at the Git tag `loop-v12`. Its control tables, training sequence, model protocol and counts are historical.

Version 0.4.0, Godot 4.7.2 (GL Compatibility), 640 x 360 pixel art shown at whole multiples
(the window opens at 1280 x 720). One night at the counter of a back-street implant clinic:
five visitors drawn from a cast of ten, one decision each, and two of them carrying something
for the gang that runs the place.

Kessler fired you after twelve years of fitting implants in its towers and sold your debt to
Harrow Credit; you kept its spec binder. The one counter that would still take you belongs to
the Lanterns, a street gang. You compare each patient with a **reference sheet**: the binder's
normal for the implants that patient wears. You may look, run a test, ask, calm them for a
price, say something that stings, type a sentence of your own, or speak one aloud. All of it
is optional and in any order, and you can decide before doing any of it. Then you either
**TREAT** the patient or call **TRAUMA TEAM** to take them away. If a patient turns on you, you
drop the shutter.

Two of each night's five visitors are the Lanterns' **contacts**, carrying smuggled military
implants. Tonight's **contact list** on the left wall gives each one two clues, and an alias
and a code drawn for that night. Say the right code to the right visitor and the package pays
300 credits; say it to anyone else and it costs that visitor's patience.

You are squeezed too. Your chair, your tools and your own two hands are on lease from Harrow,
and its meter reads your till once an hour: every patient is one hour, and every hour the till
has to be 400 further along. A night that closes under that line is the last one. Five right
calls alone do not hold it: the night needs at least one drop.

The screen is the inside of your booth: one large window with the patient across the counter,
and everything else (the sheet, the list, your notes, the hall camera, the controls) a thing
on the walls or the desk that you click.

Everything on screen and in the speakers is generated by the scripts in `tools/art`, except
the Chinese glyphs, which are cut unchanged from Fusion Pixel Font (SIL Open Font License 1.1;
Languages, below). Nothing is taken from another game.

The game is in English or Chinese (中文): choose it under SETTINGS on the title card, or with
the switch in its top-right corner.

## Launch

From the repository root, in PowerShell:

```powershell
.\run-game.ps1
```

The script looks for Godot in `$env:GODOT_BIN`, then in
`%LOCALAPPDATA%\CodexTools\Godot\4.7.2\`, then on `PATH`. Or pass `-GodotPath <exe>`.

The game loads its pictures, fonts and sounds from Godot's import cache, `game/.godot/`,
which is not in the repository, so in a fresh clone nothing would load. Before it starts the
game, the script checks that every file the `.import` files name is in the cache and, if any is
missing, imports first (about 6 seconds from an empty cache). Godot's import from an empty
cache sometimes crashes (Checks, below); a second import finished the one crashed import it was
tried on, so the script tries up to three times and otherwise says to open `game/project.godot`
in the editor once.

| Option | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| `-Language en`, `-Language zh` | use this language for the run |
| `-ImportOnly` | import if anything is missing, then stop without starting the game |
| `-Patient script`, `mock`, `timeout`, `live` or a service URL | choose the patient for this run. Mock and timeout start and stop a local service. Live prompts for a paid launch. See the [proxy runbook](../agent_proxy/README.md). |
| `-AgentUrl http://127.0.0.1:8787 -Model` | patients speak through the proxy in `../agent_proxy` (start it first: `npm run dev`) |
| `-LessBlood` | the attack scenes without blood: no splatter, pools or stains, and a clean face, hand, shutter cut and broken glass. The red flash and the red edge of the screen stay |
| `-Mute` | start with sound off (M toggles it in the game) |
| `-Seed 123` | fixed seed for the night (who comes, who of them drifts and which signs they show), the order of the visitors, the night's contact list and the small random timings |
| `-TestNight C` | that test night of `data/nights.json` (A to J) every shift, instead of a drawn night (The patients, below) |

Without the script, import once (`godot --headless --path game --import`, again if it stops
with an error), then: `godot --path game -- --agent-url=http://127.0.0.1:8787 --model --less-blood --mute --seed=123 --test-night=C`
(everything after `--` is optional). The environment variable `BTC_AGENT_URL` also sets the
address. These have no script option:

| Game option | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| `--authored-order` | test night A every shift, the night as written (the tests and the capture script use it) |
| `--no-intro` | no opening cards, no first-night training and no counter guide opening by itself |
| `--no-training` | the opening cards, but no training: the counter guide opens by itself at the first patient instead, as before November 17 |
| `--no-surprises` | no snaps and no flares (the tests and the capture script use it and cue the ones they need) |
| `--lang=zh`, `--lang=en` | this language for the run, over the choice saved from the title card (the tests and the capture script use it; it is not saved) |

On November 15 the script was run once by an agent (offline, title screen, closed with the
window's close message): the game started and left cleanly. That was the build before the

## Languages

[Student, November 18: a language option on the title screen, Chinese first, with everything
that should be translated translated; and if one side is changed, the other must be changed
too.]

The language is chosen on the title card: LANGUAGE on its SETTINGS page, or **English | 中文**
in its top-right corner (The title card, below). A click switches every word the game shows,
and the choice is kept for the next start (`user://settings.cfg`). The language changes only on
the title card, where nothing is half-said.

- **What is translated.** Everything the player reads: the opening, the training, the guide,
  the sheet, the list, the intake card, every case's lines, the hints, the plates, the slip and
  the report. Words painted into pictures too: ten pictures have a Chinese twin,
  `<name>_zh.png` (the hall camera's label on the booth wall, the EXIT light, the shutter's
  stencil, the four things on the pinboards and the three imprints on the intake card), and
  the title sign reads 先别下刀 ("don't cut yet") over a small BEFORE THE CUT.
- **What stays English.** People's names and code names: Kip, Juno, the contacts' aliases, the
  model names on the sheet (KITE, VX-9), the key names. Companies, gangs and organizations are
  translated: Kessler is 凯斯勒, Harrow Credit 哈罗信贷, the Lanterns 灯笼帮, Trauma Team 创伤小组.
- **How.** Every word is written in English, in `data/` and in the scripts. The Chinese is a
  set of catalogs in `data/lang/zh/`, keyed by that exact English (`scripts/core/lang.gd`), and
  text is translated where it is drawn, so the rules, the data and the event log stay English.
  The Chinese glyphs are Fusion Pixel Font's 10-pixel cut (`assets/fonts/zh.fnt`, with its
  licence beside it), the fallback of every Counter Sans font: Latin letters and digits stay
  Counter Sans in both languages. Chinese is typed out and held at half the English rate per
  character, since a Chinese line says as much in about half the characters (`Lang.PER_CHAR`).
- **Typing.** While the typing line is open in Chinese, the game turns the system's input
  method on, places its candidate window at the caret and shows what is being composed there;
  the line takes hanzi and Chinese punctuation. Otherwise the input method is off, so the
  letter keys stay the game's keys.
- **The patients.** The model is asked to answer in the player's language (the proxy's
  `language` field), keeping names and code names in Latin letters, and in Chinese with the
  smaller limits of `agent.languages` in `rules.json` (55 characters a line, 110 a reply).
  Speech is transcribed in that language. The offline patient's lines are in the case catalogs,
  and it listens for Chinese words as well as English ones (`data/lang/zh/topics.json`).
- **The intake card in Chinese.** A hanzi is nine pixels tall where a capital is seven, so lines
  set nine pixels apart touch. In Chinese a row's one or two notes are set ten apart. A row
  with three notes still sets them nine apart: they touch, but stay clear of the rule below.
  The findings left the card late on November 18: on the report's visit pages, in both
  languages, NORMAL or BROKEN LINE (正常 or 异常) starts each finding's text and "you did not
  check" (你没有检查) stands on the right.
- **Keeping the two in step.** A change to the English is a change to the Chinese, in the same
  commit. `python tools/check_lang.py` fails on a line with no translation, a translation whose
  English has changed, two translations of one line, a line and its translation with different
  `{slots}` or `%`-codes, a translation that is empty or the English again, and a picture
  without its twin. `measure_text.py --lang zh`
  measures every line as it reads in Chinese, and the scene test run with ` -- --lang=zh` fails
  on any English that reaches the screen untranslated. The glossary, the patients' voices and
  how a translation is added are in
  [chinese-translation.md](../docs/development/chinese-translation.md).

## The title card

[Student, November 20: where is the language chosen? The opening guidance is still not
intuitive: a player may not know that a patient can attack without warning. The start screen
could have options such as settings, start and guide.]

Under the title and its line about the Lanterns' clinic is a menu of four: **START**, **HOW TO
PLAY**, **SETTINGS** and **QUIT**. UP and DOWN (or W and S) move the marker, the pointer lights
the item it is on, and a click or ENTER opens the lit one. SPACE, the shutter's key, does
nothing here. Under the menu a red box headed **SUDDEN ATTACKS** says what a new player could
not guess: "A drifting visitor can lunge at the glass without warning. The moment a red bar
starts to fill, press SPACE: the shutter drops." The last opening card says it again ("SPACE
drops the SHUTTER"). Until November 20 a click anywhere on the title card started the night,
and the language was a small switch in its corner.

**HOW TO PLAY** is five pages, each a title, a small picture drawn in code (none on the last)
and three to six points, with the night's numbers filled in from `rules.json`
(`data/intro.json`, `howto`):

1. **THE JOB.** Five visitors, one an hour, buzzed in from the hall camera; the reference sheet
   and its rows (eyes, hands, port, speech, temper), to compare in any order; TREAT for the
   steady, Trauma Team for two broken lines; nothing told before closing.
2. **WHEN ONE TURNS ON YOU.** A drifting visitor can turn mid-exam with no warning, likelier the
   more is done with them; the red bar under the window, and SPACE at once (or the SHUT lever);
   each notch a blow on the glass, which takes three a night, and with less glass left they come
   through sooner, which ends the night; a shout or a slammed hand with no red bar is only
   temper, and the shutter on a steady visitor costs 100 at closing; a second PRESS breaks
   anyone. The picture plays an attack over and over, slower than the real one: the bar fills,
   SPACE goes down before the first notch, the shutter drops and the plate reads STOPPED
   CLEAN: THE GLASS HOLDS.
3. **PATIENCE AND THE TILL.** The lamps and what a tool, a question and PRESS cost; CALM and its
   price; Harrow's hourly line and the reading at closing that decides; glass for 40 between
   visitors.
4. **TONIGHT'S CONTACTS.** Two a night; the list's aliases, clues and codes, new every night;
   SAY, the price of a wrong code, and a drop that says nothing about the patient.
5. **KEYS.** The mouse, the keys of the papers and the camera, typing and TAB, SPACE twice
   outside an attack, ENTER, K, M and F11, plus F10 to confirm leaving a night.

A click, ENTER or RIGHT turns to the next page, and past the last one goes back to the menu;
LEFT goes back a page; ESC, BACKSPACE, H or a right-click goes back to the menu. H or F1 opens
HOW TO PLAY from the title card. A point about the price of glass is left out under rules with
no price for it.

**SETTINGS** offers LANGUAGE (English, 中文), BLOOD (FULL, LESS), SOUND (OFF, 25%, 50%, 75%, 100%),
SCREEN (WINDOW, FULL SCREEN), PATIENT AI (OFF, ON) and BACK. PATIENT AI checks the service
address, protocol and case versions before the next visit. With no compatible service, the scripted patient answers. UP and DOWN light a row; LEFT and RIGHT
or a click choose, and ENTER takes the next choice (on BACK, it goes back); a line under the
rows says what the lit row does. Each choice applies at once and is kept for the next start in
`user://settings.cfg` (section `[game]`: `language`, `blood`, `volume` from 0 to 4, `screen`, `patient_ai`;
`scripts/core/settings.gd`). M mutes and unmutes anywhere and is not kept; while it is on,
MUTED [M] stands beside the sound row, and choosing a level there unmutes. F11 switches the
screen anywhere, and that is kept. `--less-blood` and `--mute` (`-LessBlood`, `-Mute`) hold
for one run: a saved LESS takes the blood out of a run, but a saved FULL never puts it back
against `--less-blood`. A run that pins its language, as the tests and the capture script do,
neither reads the file nor writes it. **QUIT** closes the game the way the window's close
button does. The small **English | 中文** switch in the corner stays.

The menu, the five pages, their pictures and words in both languages, the warning and the
settings were written and drawn by an assistant on November 20 in answer to the student's
request. Nobody has played them or read them in the window.

## The opening, the training and the guide

**The opening.** Once a session, after START on the title card, six short cards with small moving
pictures tell the story: FIRED (Kessler walks you out; you keep its spec binder), THE DEBT
(Kessler sells what you owe to Harrow Credit, which leases you your chair, your tools and your
own hands), THE LANTERNS (their back-street clinic, their contacts, tonight's list and what a
drop pays), THE LINE (400 an hour), THE DRIFT (street implants are illegal, cheap and sometimes
torn out of a soldier; some who wear them drift and can turn without warning, most are only
tired, scared or angry) and BEFORE THE CUT (check them against the binder; TREAT, TRAUMA TEAM,
and SPACE for the SHUTTER). Any click or ENTER moves on; ESC skips the rest.

[Student, November 18: the opening animation had hardly changed, though the story had.] The
pictures were redrawn for the story on November 18. FIRED is a strip of three panels: the badge
refused on Kessler's floor 88, the lift down between two guards, and outside in the rain, still
holding the binder, tabbed EYES, HANDS, PORTS and VOICE. THE DEBT is Kessler's staff debt file
stamped SOLD TO HARROW, with Harrow's lease slid over it. THE LANTERNS tags the matched patient
CONTACT and the parcel PACKAGE. THE DRIFT is a crate of military surplus with its serials filed
off and, through the glass, a visitor whose eye implant glitches before they crack the pane. No
picture shows a member of the cast, and the list on the Lanterns card has two made-up entries
whose clues fit nobody in it (a rule test checks them), so it cannot be taken for a real
night's list.

**The first-night training.** [Student, November 17: the grip and the eye test are confusing;
the opening guidance should be clearer, ideally walking through everything once.] On the first
night of a session a small TRAINING box gives 14 tips, one at a time, each with a pulsing
frame round the one thing it is about: the contact list, how its clues work, the hall camera,
the intake card, saying a code, the drop, the reference sheet, the penlight, the grip, the
port, the talk pads with CALM's price, the marks, the decision and, on the first slip, a last
tip about snaps and flares. Every tip has **NEXT**, and **SKIP ALL [K]** ends the training.
Nothing blocks and nothing sets an order: a tip for something already done is passed over, a
tip that is only read gives way to the next action after 2 seconds, one that asks for
something after 10. The box keeps off the open papers, the slip, the big camera screen and the
patient's eyes, and steps out of the way while the guide or a close-up is open. It runs from
the end of the opening cards until the second visitor is buzzed in. On that night the first
visitor is always on the contact list, so the first visit can show how a code works, and that
visit has no snap or flare.

**The counter guide.** Four pages dim the screen and frame one part of the booth each (the
window, the left wall, the right wall, the desk), naming what each thing there is for. H, F1
or the COUNTER GUIDE booklet on the right wall opens it, between patients or during a visit
(only F1 while you are typing). It opens by itself at the first patient only when the training
is off (`--no-training`). While it is open, a paper that was down hangs back on its wall,
where the guide points at it, and comes down again when the guide closes.

Neither the cards, the training nor the guide is a step you have to take. Five hints appear
once each when they first matter: the first note on the card, the last patience lamp, the end
of the first attack, the first drop ("The drop changes nothing about the exam. Steady or
drifting, the call is still yours.") and the first flare ("Only temper: no red bar, nothing to
stop. The SHUTTER is for the red bar."). The words are in `data/intro.json`.

## The screen

[Student, November 16: for immersion, keep only the counter on screen, as one large view of it
with the visitor across it, and turn everything else into things on the desk or the walls
that open when clicked.]

The counter window takes most of the screen (440 x 300 of the 640 x 360 pixels, with the view
through it drawn at twice its earlier size). Two strips of booth wall and the desk frame it.
Nothing is a panel: everything besides the window is an object in the booth, and a paper is
in front of the window only while you have taken it down.

- **Window** (centre): the patient at the glass. Move the pointer over their eyes, hands or
  port and click for a closer look. Looking is free. A tool fills the window with a close-up.
  The patient's **patience** lamps hang at the top of the window. The three lamps of the
  **glass**, what the pane can still take tonight, sit at its bottom right. The dark slot in
  the sill is the timing bar during an attack.
- **Left wall**, from the top:
  - the **reference sheet** and tonight's **contact list**, on one hook. Click the sheet or
    press R, click the list or press L, and that paper comes down in front of the left part of
    the window. Since both hang on the same hook, taking one down puts the other back; the
    paper that is down has a small swap tab (marked L or R) for the other, and its own tab, its
    key again or a right-click on it hangs it back. The sheet's corners blink until it has been
    down once.
  - the lender's **meter**: the till, the hour (one patient is one hour), the line the meter
    wants when this hour ends, and a lamp for each finished visit in the colour of what you did
    (green TREAT, amber Trauma Team, blue shutter, red when a patient got through, dark when
    nothing was done). The lamps say what you did, never whether it was right.
  - the **hall camera**: a small monitor that shows who is waiting. Click it, or press C, and a
    larger screen lights up in the lower left corner of the window. Between patients a second
    press, or a click on that screen, buzzes the next one in; ENTER does it at once, without
    the screen. During a visit the monitor is only a look at who waits next.
- **Right wall**, from the top:
  - the **intake card** on its clipboard: this patient's record and your notes on them. Click
    it, or press N, and it comes down in front of the right part of the window. Notes land on
    it by themselves as you look, test and talk, also while it hangs on the wall, where a
    small number counts the notes you have not looked at yet. The small box at each row is
    yours: click once for a tick, again for a cross, a third time to clear it. The card counts
    your crosses against the house rule (`CROSSED 1/2`). Nothing scores a mark, and nothing on
    the card ever says normal or abnormal: the findings are told at closing, on the report.
  - the **COUNTER GUIDE** booklet (click it, or H or F1).
  - a plate with four controls: the TREAT stamp, the TRAUMA call box, the SHUT lever and the
    GLASS key.
- **Desk** (bottom): the three instruments on the left, the three ways of talking on the
  right, and between them the transcript unit: what is said, the question list, your own
  typing line, and a hint line.

The HOUSE RULE sign that hung under the intake card until November 17 ("THEY TURN? DROP THE
SHUTTER. [SPACE]") is gone: the booklet took its place, so that the list could hang under the
sheet. The guide's right-wall page, the last training tip and the opening's last card say
what the shutter is for.

come back after it. The intake card comes down by itself when it is needed: while the TREAT

nothing hangs in front of the window. Within a shift

Repeating the request while sealed costs nothing. CALM clears the refusal. [Student, November 17: either no CALM, or CALM costs money.] CALM stays
and costs money: the pad wears its price (`30cr`), the slip prints `CALM -30` and the report

because a patient who runs out of patience leaves unpaid: CALM is now a trade of credits for a
longer exam, not a free refill.

**When patience runs out, the patient leaves.** The action that spends the last lamp still
happens. Then the patient says they are going, the lamps give way to a red LEAVING bar that
runs for eight seconds, and they walk out by themselves: no fee, no fine, not a right call,
and the hour is gone. You can still decide while the bar runs. CALM during it gives one lamp
back, for its 30 credits, and keeps them at the counter. The bar stands still while an answer
is on its way, while a test or the microphone is open, and while the guide is up.

**The second PRESS spends every remaining lamp.** Both pushes require at least two lamps. The first costs two. After it the pad reads BREAK and its hint shows what each visitor will do. A steady visitor walks out without a fee. A drifting visitor lunges. Press SPACE to drop the shutter. A successful catch triggered by this push adds 150 to the visit at closing. A visitor who runs out of patience by another action still gets the LEAVING bar.

**Snaps and flares.** [Student, November 17: during the eye test, the grip or any other
action, the patient may suddenly fly into a rage and lunge, with transitions.]

- **Who.** Only a drifting patient snaps. Anyone may flare, at most once a visit. A snap that
  comes up wins over a flare. Neither happens on the training visit.
- **When.** Each action of the doctor's is rolled once when it has run its course: a tool, a
  question, pressure, CALM, a code, the doctor's own words. Looking is free and is not an
  action. A snap's chance is (actions - 3) x 0.08, never above 0.30: nothing for the first
  three actions, then 8, 16, 24 and 30 per cent. Computed from these numbers, a drifting
  patient snaps in about one exam in five of five actions, two in five of six and seven in ten
  of eight. A flare's chance is 0.10 for every action after the first.
- **How.** What came up waits 0.6 to 1.6 seconds (a flare 0.5 to 1.4), so it lands inside
  whatever the doctor is doing by then, though never in a reply on its way, a line still being
  typed, the typing line, the microphone or the guide. A close-up is no shelter: it is knocked
  out of the doctor's hands (it jolts, drops and fades). A snap is a surge at the glass with a
  bang, a shake and a red flash, and the attack starts 0.35 seconds later (`snap.lead_ms`)
  instead of after the usual beat; the red bar, the blows and the glass are those of any
  attack. Caught, a snap is a right call; missed, it is a hit (-300).
- **A flare** is a shout and a hand slammed on the glass, with a smaller surge and shake, but
  no red bar and nothing to stop. It is evidence for the TEMPER line only, and it agrees with
  the case: a steady patient's face and voice go together, a drifting patient's do not.
  Dropping the shutter on it is a false alarm (-100).
- Tying the snap to the number of actions gives it a rule a player can learn: a long exam of a
  drifting patient is dangerous, so knowing when to stop examining and decide is part of the
  game. The flare is there so that a shout at the glass is not proof by itself.

## Saying it in your own words

**Typing.** Press T, ENTER on a quiet strip, or TYPE. ENTER submits the line, CTRL+ENTER sends it as PRESS and ESC cancels. The model routes each line into one of ten intents. A plausible case question shows its wording and lamp price before a paid ASK. Choose the listed question, pick free chat, or choose a different question. A repeat after a successful answer is free. Unclear input also offers the free and question-menu choices. The offline patient recognizes English and Chinese keywords. Ordinary talk and insults sent as speech cost no patience and reveal no case evidence.

**Push-to-talk.** Offered only when a patient AI service is connected (`-AgentUrl ... -Model`).
Hold TAB, or hold the mouse on the SPEAK button, and talk; let go to stop. The title card says
which way of talking is on tonight ("Patient AI on: ... Hold TAB to speak, or press T to type."
or "Patient AI off: the authored patient answers. ..."), and offline TAB shows a hint that
says what speaking needs.

- The microphone is open only while the key is held. Nothing listens otherwise.
- While it is open the patient looks at you and their hands stop.
- Opening it takes about half a second on Windows (measured November 15). Until it is ready
  the strip says OPENING THE MICROPHONE...; speak when it says LISTENING. Letting go before
  that sends nothing and says so. The game keeps running while it opens.
- The strip shows a level meter and a clock while listening, then "writing it down".
- The words arrive **on the typing line**. Nothing is sent on key release: you read them,
  correct them if needed, then use ENTER to speak, CTRL+ENTER to press, or ESC to discard them.
- A clip is 0.35 to 10 seconds, at most 24 per visit. It is kept in memory only, sent once to
  the proxy and dropped. Neither the clip nor the transcript is written to any log.
- A tap, silence, no microphone, no words, a slow or failed service and a full budget each
  show a short hint and leave whatever was typed. Typing and subtitles always work.
- ESC, right-click, losing window focus, a decision or the next patient cancel a clip. A
  transcript that arrives late for a cancelled clip or a previous patient is ignored.
- Game sound is turned down while the microphone is open.
- The patient does not speak aloud; replies are subtitles.

## Patient AI modes

- **Offline comparison** (default): authored lines and choices from the case files. No network.
- **Model** (`--agent-url=... --model`). A typed or confirmed spoken line goes first to a short topic classifier. A matching question follows the ASK button's price, evidence and possibility of a first dodge. Other conversation costs no patience and makes no note. ASK uses the case's authored answer by default. The model writes other replies and may decide a first request for an unsealed instrument. CTRL+ENTER sends the typing line as PRESS. The corner shows the reply source and the 16-reply allowance. Each arrival opens one session. The patient remembers the conversation and actions in order, including a grip gauge opened or completed and each PRESS. Leaving the chair clears that record, even when the same character returns. An arrival, a completed instrument check or 12 quiet seconds may prompt a short optional line. Only three optional attempts (including at most two idle attempts) fit inside the 16. A player action cancels a pending optional line.
- **Codes are game-owned.** Saying a code makes no model call and adds nothing to the
  patient's conversation memory, so the patient AI can never confirm, deny or leak one.
- **The player's language** goes with every request. In Chinese the model is asked for
  Simplified Chinese with names and code names in Latin letters, within the smaller Chinese
  limits; the readings, the codes and the scoring do not change with the language.
- **SPEECH and TEMPER** enter the dialogue prompt when their evidence becomes available. An answered question outside a broken SPEECH sign's evidence list reveals the normal sign. PRESS or a flare opens TEMPER. Every other instrument reading stays inside the game.
- If a call fails, is late (4 s) or the budget is spent, the authored line is used and labelled.
- A quarter of a second after a question has gone to the service, the patient is seen thinking
  it over: each looks away in their own manner, with a small thought bubble by the head, until
  the answer comes. The pose follows the patient's habit, never whether they are steady or
  drifting. Offline the answer is there at once and no pose is shown.
- While a reply is on its way, or its first line is still typing, you can look, take a paper
  down or hang it back, mark the card, switch the hall screen on, pick up the TREAT stamp, open
  the Trauma Team line and use the shutter. A look shows its own line and keeps the dots away
  for 1.6 s. TREAT or the call decide at once and drop the reply (`reply_dropped` in the event
  log). Asking, tools, CALM, PRESS, codes, typing and speaking wait; after 0.8 s the strip says
  "Still answering. You can look meanwhile, or decide now."

From a checkout with its configured project key, `.\run-game.ps1 -Language zh -TestNight K` starts Godot with the local OpenAI service. The title checks the connection before opening a visit. A fresh standalone game starts offline. Enabling PATIENT AI in SETTINGS uses the configured address or `http://127.0.0.1:8787`. Select `-Patient mock` for the synthetic demo. (`%APPDATA%\Godot\app_userdata\Before the Cut\logs`). It holds a random session id, case ids,
actions, timings and results. Typed sentences, spoken clips and transcripts are not logged;
only their lengths and stages are. Since November 16 it also holds the meter's readings
(`meter`), the player's marks as a row and a value (`mark`), a patient running out of
patience (`spent`, `leaving`, `gave_up`), the glass left at each attack and the grade of each
stop (`attack`, `shutter`), the opening and the guide (`intro`, `guide`), every paper or
screen taken down or put back (`panel`: which one, and whether it is out), each layer of
glass paid for (`glass_repair`: the cost, the glass left and the till after it), and the
night's glass money in `shift_end` (`glass_paid`). Since November 17: the night's list
(`contacts`: how many entries, their ids, and whether it is the training night), each code
said (`code`: the entry, right or wrong, its cost and the patience left), CALM's price and the
patches used (`reassure`: `cost`, `calm_spent`), a snap or flare that came up and when it is
due (`surprise_due`), the snap and the flare themselves (`snap`, `flare`, with the number of
actions so far), and the training (`training`, `training_end`: skipped or not, how many tips
it reached). Since November 18: the language of the session (`language` in `session_start`) and

## Known limits

- The game's own service ran 1,094 bilingual evaluation requests against GPT-6 Luna on November 21. E2 missed the evidence threshold, especially for normal speech in Chinese, so graded ASK uses its authored answers. The corrected classifier, price confirmation and optional turns have been exercised in Godot. Human role continuity, whether patients prompt follow-ups, physical microphone quality and Chinese transcription accuracy still need outside players. November 15's ten real transcriptions used synthetic speech. [Full results and sample locations](../HANDOFF.md#full-build-november-21)

- The Chinese was written by an assistant and has not been read by a person: not the
  translation, not its fit on screen in play, and not the patients' voices in it
  ([glossary and voices](../docs/development/chinese-translation.md)). Typing Chinese has been
  checked only by the scene test, which hands the typing line what an input method would (the
  characters, and the text still being composed); no one has typed with a real input method in
  the game window, so where its candidate list appears and how it behaves is unknown.
- In Chinese, a row of the intake card with three notes sets them nine pixels apart, so they
  touch. A Chinese line may break inside a word (常 | 见), which Chinese allows but a careful
  typesetter would avoid.
- The counter-first screen was designed by an assistant from a two-line request of the
  student; the student's notes of November 17 are about it, and the answers to them have not
  been reviewed. What hangs where, the one hook shared by the sheet and the list, the keys R,
  L, N, C, G and K, the card that comes down by itself for the stamp, and the
  transcript unit in the desk are all proposals. A paper that is down covers the top of one
  side of the window (132 of its 440 pixels in width); whether that is comfortable in play is
  unknown.

  400 an hour for the line, a bounty equal to the patient's fee, the draw's weights and its
