# Before the Cut: playable prototype

Version 0.3.0, Godot 4.7.2 (GL Compatibility), 640 x 360 pixel art shown at whole multiples
(the window opens at 1280 x 720). One night at the counter of a back-street implant clinic:
five patients, one decision each.

You compare each patient with a **normal reference sheet**. You may look, run a test, ask,
reassure, say something that stings, type a sentence of your own, or speak one aloud. All of
it is optional and in any order, and you can decide before doing any of it. Then you either
**TREAT** the patient or call **TRAUMA TEAM** to take them away. If a patient turns on you,
you drop the shutter.

You are squeezed too. Your chair, your tools and your own two hands are on lease from a
lender, and its meter reads your till once an hour: every patient is one hour, and every hour
the till has to be 120 further along. A night that closes under that line is the last one.

The screen is the inside of your booth: one large window with the patient across the counter,
and everything else (the sheet, your notes, the hall camera, the controls) a thing on the
walls or the desk that you click.

Everything on screen and in the speakers is generated by the scripts in `tools/art`. Nothing
is taken from another game.

> were exercised in the game window; the other eighteen were HTTP probes. See the

## Launch

From the repository root, in PowerShell:

```powershell
.\run-game.ps1
```

The script looks for Godot in `$env:GODOT_BIN`, then in
`%LOCALAPPDATA%\CodexTools\Godot\4.7.2\`, then on `PATH`. Or pass `-GodotPath <exe>`.

| Option | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| `-AgentUrl http://127.0.0.1:8787 -Model` | patients speak through the proxy in `../agent_proxy` (start it first: `npm run dev`) |
| `-LessBlood` | the attack scenes without blood: no splatter, pools or stains, and a clean face, hand, shutter cut and broken glass. The red flash and the red edge of the screen stay |
| `-Mute` | start with sound off (M toggles it in the game) |
| `-Seed 123` | fixed seed for the small random timings |

Without the script: `godot --path game -- --agent-url=http://127.0.0.1:8787 --model --less-blood --mute --seed=123`
(everything after `--` is optional). The environment variable `BTC_AGENT_URL` also sets the address.
`--authored-order` (no script option) keeps the patients in the order `rules.json` lists them;
the tests and the capture script use it. `--no-intro` (no script option) starts a session
without the opening cards and without the counter guide opening by itself.

On November 15 the script was run once by an agent (offline, title screen, closed with the
window's close message): the game started and left cleanly. That was the build before the
changes of November 16. The current build has booted headlessly in both modes and has been
drawn on a hidden desktop; nobody has started it in a visible window yet.

## The opening and the guide

Once a session, after the title card, five short cards with small moving pictures say who you
are, what you owe and what the job is. Any click or ENTER moves on; ESC skips the rest. When
the first patient of a session reaches the counter, a four-page guide dims the screen, frames
one part of the booth on each page (the window, the left wall, the right wall, the desk) and
names what each thing there is for. H, F1 or the COUNTER GUIDE booklet on the left wall opens
it again, between patients or during a visit (only F1 while you are typing). While it is open,
a paper that was down hangs back on its wall, where the guide points at it, and comes down
again when the guide closes. Neither the cards nor the guide is a step you have to take. Three
hints appear once each when they first matter: the first note on the card, the last patience
lamp, the end of the first attack. The words are in `data/intro.json`; `--no-intro` turns the
cards and the guide's first showing off.

## The screen

with the visitor across it, and turn everything else into things on the desk or the walls
that open when clicked.]

The counter window takes most of the screen (440 x 300 of the 640 x 360 pixels, with the view
through it drawn at twice its earlier size). Two strips of booth wall and the desk frame it.
Nothing is a panel: everything besides the window is an object in the booth, and a paper is
in front of the window only while you have taken it down.

- **Window** (centre): the patient at the glass. Move the pointer over their eyes, hands or
  port and click for a closer look. Looking is free. A tool fills the window with a close-up.
  The patient's **patience** lamps hang at the top of the window. The three lamps of the
  **glass**, what the pane can still take tonight, sit at its bottom right. The dark slot in
  the sill is the timing bar during an attack.
- **Left wall**, from the top:
  - the **reference sheet** on its nail. Click it, or press R, and it comes down in front of
    the left part of the window; its tab, R again or a right-click on it hangs it back. Its
    corners blink until it has been down once.
  - the **COUNTER GUIDE** booklet (click it, or H or F1).
  - the lender's **meter**: the till, the hour (one patient is one hour), the line the meter
    wants when this hour ends, and a tick or cross for how each finished call went.
  - the **hall camera**: a small monitor that shows who is waiting. Click it, or press C, and a
    larger screen lights up in the lower left corner of the window. Between patients a second
    press, or a click on that screen, buzzes the next one in; ENTER does it at once, without
    the screen. During a visit the monitor is only a look at who waits next.
- **Right wall**, from the top:
  - the **intake card** on its clipboard: this patient's record and your notes on them. Click
    it, or press N, and it comes down in front of the right part of the window. Notes land on
    it by themselves as you look, test and talk, also while it hangs on the wall, where a
    small number counts the notes you have not looked at yet. The small box at each row is
    yours: click once for a tick, again for a cross, a third time to clear it. The card counts
    your crosses against the house rule (`CROSSED 1/2`). Nothing scores a mark, and nothing on
    the card says normal or abnormal until the visit is over.
  - the **HOUSE RULE** sign: "THEY TURN? DROP THE SHUTTER. [SPACE]".
  - a plate with four controls: the TREAT stamp, the TRAUMA call box, the SHUT lever and the
    GLASS key.
- **Desk** (bottom): the three instruments on the left, the three ways of talking on the
  right, and between them the transcript unit: what is said, the question list, your own
  typing line, and a hint line.

**What is in front of what.** A paper that is down takes the pointer before the patient does:
a click on it is never passed on to what is behind it. The middle of the window stays clear
with both papers down. The papers step aside by themselves for a tool close-up and come back
after it. The intake card comes down by itself when it is needed: while the TREAT stamp is in
your hand, for 1.3 seconds after the stamp lands, and with the findings when the visit is
over. Once a patient has turned, nothing hangs in front of the window. Within a shift the
papers stay where you left them from one patient to the next; a new shift starts with both on
the wall. Until a patient turns, papers and screens behave the same whatever that patient is
about to do.

| Pad | Where | What it does | Costs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| LIGHT | desk | penlight: sweep it over both eyes | 1 patience to ask, free once allowed |
| GRIP | desk | grip bar: hold the mouse button, then let go to say "release" | 1 patience to ask, free once allowed |
| PORT | desk | a close look at the socket | 1 patience to ask if it is covered, otherwise free |
| ASK | desk | a short list of questions | 1 patience for a new answer |
| CALM | desk | reassure: +1 patience and a refusal softens; twice per visit | nothing |
| PRESS | desk | say something that stings and watch the reaction | 2 patience |
| TREAT | right wall | pick up the stamp, then stamp the intake card | decision |
| TRAUMA | right wall | press twice: the line opens, then the call goes out | decision |
| SHUT | right wall | the shutter: ends the visit at once | decision |
| GLASS | right wall | between patients: one new layer for the counter glass | 40 credits |

A patient may refuse a test or give a reason instead. Insisting costs 1 more; CALM first
clears the refusal.

**When patience runs out, the patient leaves.** The action that spends the last lamp still
happens. Then the patient says they are going, the lamps give way to a red LEAVING bar that
runs for eight seconds, and they walk out by themselves: no fee, no fine, not a right call,
and the hour is gone. You can still decide while the bar runs. CALM during it gives one lamp
back and keeps them at the counter. The bar stands still while an answer is on its way, while
a test or the microphone is open, and while the guide is up.

| --- | --- |

| ENTER | start, next opening card, buzz the next patient in, hurry a line, tear off the slip, new shift; on a quiet strip it opens the typing line |
| ESC | skip the opening cards; close the guide |
| H / F1 | the counter guide |
| R | the reference sheet: down, or back on its nail |
| N | the intake card: down, or back on its wall |
| C | the hall camera's larger screen; between patients a second press buzzes the next one in |
| G | between patients: one new layer of counter glass for 40 credits |
| T | open the typing line |
| TAB (hold) | push-to-talk, only when a patient AI service is connected |
| SPACE | the shutter: once to arm, again to drop; during an attack once is enough |
| M / F11 | mute / fullscreen |

While the typing line is open the letter keys and SPACE type; F1 still opens the guide.

## The reference sheet

| Line | Normal | Ordinary, not a sign |
| --- | --- | --- |
| EYES | Pupils tighten in light. Both eyes follow it. | Implants don't tighten. They still follow. |
| HANDS | Lets go on 'release', inside one second. | Shakes, twitches and a weak grip. |
| PORT | Calm skin at the socket. No dark threads. | Burnt pins, scars, cheap cable marks. |
| SPEECH | Says 'I' and 'my'. Remembers what they did. | Nerves, rudeness, a skipping voice box. |
| TEMPER | Pushed, face and voice react together. | Anger, swearing, walking out. |

Footer, the house rule: "Two broken lines = drifting: call TRAUMA TEAM." and "Strange is not
sick. A refusal is not a sign." The sheet is `data/sheet.json`; the two is its `threshold`.
Every drifting case breaks at least that many lines (the data check refuses a case that does
not), and no steady case breaks any. The rule is advice for the player: the game scores the
call, never the marks.

## Decisions and money

| What happened | Credits | Counts as a right call |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Treated a steady patient | their fee (250 to 350) | yes |
| Treated a drifting patient | -400 | no |
| Trauma Team took a drifting patient | +120 | yes |
| The patient turned and the shutter came down in time | +120 | yes |
| The patient turned and got through | -300 | only if it was your call to Trauma Team that set them off |
| Trauma Team called on a steady patient | -150 (the call-out is billed) | no |
| Shutter on a steady patient | -100 | no |
| Shutter on a drifting patient during the exam | 0 | yes |
| Pressed until they walked out | 0 | no |
| Out of patience: they left by themselves | 0 | no |

**The lender's line.** The meter wants 120 more in the till every hour (`line.per_hour`), so
hour n needs n x 120 and the night needs 600. After each slip the meter reads the till against
the line for that hour: over, or UNDER THE LINE in red. A reading under the line is counted
and shown in the report, but the shift goes on; only the last reading decides the night.
Closing under the line is the ending called THE CUT, whatever else went right. 120 is also
what a right call on a drifting patient pays, so that call exactly holds the line for its hour.

Five right calls make 1,140. Treating everyone makes 100 and calling Trauma Team on everyone
makes -210: both end under the line. One needless call still holds it (640), two do not (190).
These totals are asserted by the rule tests, and so is that every one of the 1,024 combinations
of calls ends in a titled report. The one thing the till can be spent on is new counter glass
(below). All numbers are first-pass values in `data/rules.json` that no player has tuned.

**When a patient turns** there is a warning line and a short beat. Then a red bar in the slot
of the sill fills from the warning to the moment they are through; its notches are the blows
on the glass. SPACE (or the SHUT lever) drops the shutter, and when you do it matters:

| The shutter comes down | The plate says | The glass |
| --- | --- | --- |
| before the first blow | STOPPED CLEAN: THE GLASS HOLDS | untouched |
| after one or two blows | STOPPED AFTER 1 BLOW / 2 BLOWS | one lamp out per blow |
| after the blow that takes the last lamp | STOPPED, BUT THE GLASS IS GONE | none left |
| in time, with no glass left to hit | STOPPED IN TIME: NO GLASS LEFT | none left |
| not in time | they come through | none left |

The pane takes three blows a night (`attack.glass`), and what it has taken stays until you pay
to have a layer put back or the shift ends. With whole glass the blows land 0.54, 0.99 and
1.44 seconds after the warning and the patient is through at 1.8. Less glass means fewer blows
before the break-through: 1.44 seconds with two lamps left, 0.99 with one, 0.65 with none. So
a sloppy stop on the first attacker makes the second one faster. A pane that has lost its last
lamp drops out of its frame behind the shutter, and the frame stays empty until you pay for a
layer; the next shift starts with a whole pane either way. Trauma Team is an authored outcome
with fixed lines; it is not another AI agent.

**Mending the glass.** [Student, November 16: also let the player pay to repair the counter's
blast glass when earlier visitors have damaged it.] Between patients, while the next one waits
in the hall, the GLASS key on the right wall (or G) orders one layer. It costs 40 credits
(`attack.repair_cost`; 0 switches repairs off) and puts one lamp back, up to the three of a
whole pane; an empty frame gets glass again. The key shows its price while a layer is missing
and can be ordered, and pointing at it says why when it cannot (a patient is at the counter,
or every layer is whole). The money comes off the till at once, and the till may go under zero
for it. The meter counts it at every later reading; readings already taken do not change. The
report shows what was paid ("Till, after 120 paid for glass"). A whole pane from an empty
frame costs 120, exactly one hour of the line, so safety is bought with the same credits the
lender wants. The arm that fits the layer wipes the pane as it goes; blood on the sill and the
booth walls and the damage to the shutter stay until the shift ends. The price, the debt and
the absence of any other limit are first guesses that nobody has played against.

## Saying it in your own words

**Typing.** T (or ENTER on a quiet strip, or the TYPE button on the strip) opens a line of up
to 120 characters. ENTER says it, ESC takes it back. Offline, the patient only knows its
script: a sentence that matches one of its questions is answered as that question, anything
else gets an honest "offline" hint and costs nothing. With a patient AI service the sentence
goes to the model as the doctor's words and the patient answers for itself; the first such
exchange costs 1 patience, the rest are free.

**Push-to-talk.** Offered only when a patient AI service is connected (`-AgentUrl ... -Model`).
Hold TAB, or hold the mouse on the SPEAK button, and talk; let go to stop. The title card says
which way of talking is on tonight ("Patient AI on: ... Hold TAB to speak, or press T to type."
or "Patient AI off: the authored patient answers. ..."), and offline TAB shows a hint that
says what speaking needs.

- The microphone is open only while the key is held. Nothing listens otherwise.
- While it is open the patient looks at you and their hands stop.
- Opening it takes about half a second on Windows (measured November 15). Until it is ready
  the strip says OPENING THE MICROPHONE...; speak when it says LISTENING. Letting go before
  that sends nothing and says so. The game keeps running while it opens.
- The strip shows a level meter and a clock while listening, then "writing it down".
- The words arrive **on the typing line**. Nothing is sent on key release: you read them,
  correct them if needed, then ENTER says them or ESC takes them back.
- A clip is 0.35 to 10 seconds, at most 24 per visit. It is kept in memory only, sent once to
  the proxy and dropped. Neither the clip nor the transcript is written to any log.
- A tap, silence, no microphone, no words, a slow or failed service and a full budget each
  show a short hint and leave whatever was typed. Typing and subtitles always work.
- ESC, right-click, losing window focus, a decision or the next patient cancel a clip. A
  transcript that arrives late for a cancelled clip or a previous patient is ignored.
- Game sound is turned down while the microphone is open.
- The patient does not speak aloud; replies are subtitles.

## Patient AI modes

- **Offline comparison** (default): authored lines and choices from the case files. No network.
- **Model** (`--agent-url=... --model`): every line and reaction is generated by the model
  behind the proxy from the character brief and the conversation so far. There is no answer
  list: the three questions under ASK are suggestions, and the answers to them are generated
  like the answer to anything you type or say. The model may also refuse a test. Instrument
  readings, evidence, attacks, scoring and Trauma Team stay with the game. The corner of the
  window shows `AI DIALOGUE`, `MOCK TRANSPORT` or `OFFLINE FALLBACK` with the calls used this
  visit (16 at most).
- If a call fails, is late (4 s) or the budget is spent, the authored line is used and labelled.
- A quarter of a second after a question has gone to the service, the patient is seen thinking
  it over: each looks away in their own manner, with a small thought bubble by the head, until
  the answer comes. The pose follows the patient's habit, never whether they are steady or
  drifting. Offline the answer is there at once and no pose is shown.
- While a reply is on its way, or its first line is still typing, you can look, take a paper
  down or hang it back, mark the card, switch the hall screen on, pick up the TREAT stamp, open
  the Trauma Team line and use the shutter. A look shows its own line and keeps the dots away
  for 1.6 s. TREAT or the call decide at once and drop the reply (`reply_dropped` in the event
  log). Asking, tools, CALM, PRESS, typing and speaking wait; after 0.8 s the strip says "Still
  answering. You can look meanwhile, or decide now."

The proxy, its limits and what a live setup needs are in `../agent_proxy/README.md`.

## Event log

One JSON line per interaction in `user://logs/session_<id>.jsonl`
(`%APPDATA%\Godot\app_userdata\Before the Cut\logs`). It holds a random session id, case ids,
actions, timings and results. Typed sentences, spoken clips and transcripts are not logged;
only their lengths and stages are. Since November 16 it also holds the meter's readings
(`meter`), the player's marks as a row and a value (`mark`), a patient running out of
patience (`spent`, `leaving`, `gave_up`), the glass left at each attack and the grade of each
stop (`attack`, `shutter`), the opening and the guide (`intro`, `guide`), every paper or
screen taken down or put back (`panel`: which one, and whether it is out), each layer of
glass paid for (`glass_repair`: the cost, the glass left and the till after it), and the
night's glass money in `shift_end` (`glass_paid`).

## Known limits

- The only live run is the one of November 15 (30 patient replies, ten transcriptions; see the
  live report linked at the top). It was made before the changes of November 16, and no paid
  service has been called since. The thinking pose, the leaving countdown, the hourly line and
  the counter-first screen have therefore never run against a live model, and one run says
  little about believability.
- The counter-first screen was designed by an assistant from a two-line request of the
  student, who has not yet reviewed it. What hangs where, which papers start the night on the
  wall, the keys R, N, C and G, the card that comes down by itself for the stamp and the
  findings, and the transcript unit in the desk are all proposals. A paper that is down covers
  the top of one side of the window (132 of its 440 pixels in width); whether that is
  comfortable in play is unknown.
- Glass repair is new and untuned: 40 credits a layer is a first guess, the till may go into
  debt for it, and nothing limits it but the three layers of the pane. Whether buying glass is
  ever worth part of an hour of the line is a question for play.
- The real microphone has been opened by the game (Windows audio driver) but nobody has spoken
  into it for a check. The ten transcriptions of November 15 were synthesized speech sent to
  the service over HTTP, not sound that came through the game's microphone path. Keyboard
  layouts and input methods are untested. A denied Windows microphone permission should show
  the "no microphone" or "heard nothing" hint; that is untested.
- Every number added on November 16 is a first guess that nobody has played against: 120 an
  hour for the line, eight seconds before a patient with no patience leaves, an attack window
  of 1.8 s with blows at 0.54, 0.99 and 1.44 s, three glass lamps a night, 0.65 s with none
  left, 40 credits for a layer, and two crosses for the house rule. The tests press the
  shutter within one frame, so nobody knows yet whether a person can make those windows, or
  whether they are too easy.
- The night cannot be lost before closing: an hour under the line costs nothing by itself, and
  only the till at the end of the fifth hour decides. The glass is whole again every shift.
- The opening cards, the guide's wording, the coach hints, the hints on the wall objects, the
  fitter's line and the lender (the invented "Harrow Credit") were written by an assistant and
  have not been reviewed by the student.
- The marks on the intake card are the player's own notes. After the visit they are shown
  beside what was true; they are never scored, and no rule reads them.
- While the patient is answering (up to 4 s with a slow service), asking, tools, CALM, PRESS,
  typing and speaking wait; looking, the papers, marking, the stamp, the call line and the
  shutter do not (above).
- The typing line has no paste. Transcripts that are not in Latin letters are refused with a hint.
- A typed provocation has no rule effect of its own: only the PRESS pad moves patience and
  can set a patient off. Exit, attack, leaving and Trauma Team lines are authored in both modes.
- The same five patients come every shift, with the same truths and the same signs; only the
  order is shuffled (and the first shift of a session opens with a steady patient). Signs are
  not recombined at random. No save, no settings screen, no export build.
- `--less-blood` was judged from the 89 frames captured on the evening of November 16, of
  which 27 were looked at, and from five picture checks and three blood counts in the scene
  test. Blood that only shows between the captured moments would have been missed.
- Closing the window stops every sound and leaves 0.2 seconds later, which ends Godot's
  `4 ObjectDB instances were leaked at exit` warning. That is checked headlessly with the dummy
  audio driver only. A run that ends the engine another way while the ambience plays, such as
  `--quit-after`, still prints the warning.
- Nobody has operated the build of November 16 in a visible window: it has run only headless
  and on a hidden desktop, driven by scripts. The build of November 15 was operated by an
  agent, through posted window messages. The sound has been measured, not listened to. One
  sound file was added on November 16, `glass_fit.wav` for the arm that fits new glass: it is
  generated and the game plays it when a layer is ordered, but the tests run with the dummy
  audio driver, its place in the mix has not been measured and nobody has heard it. The
  opening, the guide, the meter, the crossed rule, the leaving countdown and the papers reuse
  existing sounds, and how they sit in the mix has not been measured either.
  On November 15 the attack sounds were turned down 9 dB
  (`ATTACK_TRIM_DB` in `main.gd`): measured from a silent recording of the mix, the loudest
  second of an attack went from about 24 dB over the rain to 19 dB (caught) and 22 dB
  (landed), and the limiter from 21% / 59% of the time to 0% / 8%. The whine now fades in
  0.12 s when the shutter drops early, and the file ends with a 60 ms release instead of a
  cut. Whether that is loud enough, or too loud, needs a person with headphones.
- Blood from an attack stays until the shift ends. So does broken glass, unless you pay for
  new layers.
- No play session by a person is recorded for this version.
