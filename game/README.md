# Before the Cut: playable prototype

Version 0.3.0, Godot 4.7.2 (GL Compatibility), 640 x 360 pixel art shown at whole multiples
(the window opens at 1280 x 720). One night at the counter of a back-street implant clinic:
five visitors, one decision each, and two of them carrying something for the gang that runs
the place.

Kessler fired you after twelve years of fitting implants in its towers and sold your debt to
Harrow Credit; you kept its spec binder. The one counter that would still take you belongs to
the Lanterns, a street gang. You compare each patient with a **reference sheet**: the binder's
normal for the implants that patient wears. You may look, run a test, ask, calm them for a
price, say something that stings, type a sentence of your own, or speak one aloud. All of it
is optional and in any order, and you can decide before doing any of it. Then you either
**TREAT** the patient or call **TRAUMA TEAM** to take them away. If a patient turns on you, you
drop the shutter.

Two of each night's five visitors are the Lanterns' **contacts**, carrying smuggled military
implants. Tonight's **contact list** on the left wall gives each one an alias, two clues and a
code. Say the right code to the right visitor and the package pays 300 credits; say it to
anyone else and it costs that visitor's patience.

You are squeezed too. Your chair, your tools and your own two hands are on lease from Harrow,
and its meter reads your till once an hour: every patient is one hour, and every hour the till
has to be 240 further along. A night that closes under that line is the last one. Five right
calls alone do not hold it: the night needs at least one drop.

The screen is the inside of your booth: one large window with the patient across the counter,
and everything else (the sheet, the list, your notes, the hall camera, the controls) a thing
on the walls or the desk that you click.

Everything on screen and in the speakers is generated by the scripts in `tools/art`, except
the Chinese glyphs, which are cut unchanged from Fusion Pixel Font (SIL Open Font License 1.1;
Languages, below). Nothing is taken from another game.

The game is in English or Chinese (中文): the switch is on the title card.

## Launch

From the repository root, in PowerShell:

```powershell
.\run-game.ps1
```

The script looks for Godot in `$env:GODOT_BIN`, then in
`%LOCALAPPDATA%\CodexTools\Godot\4.7.2\`, then on `PATH`. Or pass `-GodotPath <exe>`.

The game loads its pictures, fonts and sounds from Godot's import cache, `game/.godot/`,
which is not in the repository, so in a fresh clone nothing would load. Before it starts the
game, the script checks that every file the `.import` files name is in the cache and, if any is
missing, imports first (about 6 seconds from an empty cache). Godot's import from an empty
cache sometimes crashes (Checks, below); a second import finished the one crashed import it was
tried on, so the script tries up to three times and otherwise says to open `game/project.godot`
in the editor once.

| Option | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| `-ImportOnly` | import if anything is missing, then stop without starting the game |
| `-AgentUrl http://127.0.0.1:8787 -Model` | patients speak through the proxy in `../agent_proxy` (start it first: `npm run dev`) |
| `-LessBlood` | the attack scenes without blood: no splatter, pools or stains, and a clean face, hand, shutter cut and broken glass. The red flash and the red edge of the screen stay |
| `-Mute` | start with sound off (M toggles it in the game) |
| `-Seed 123` | fixed seed for the small random timings, the order of the visitors and the night's contact list |

Without the script, import once (`godot --headless --path game --import`, again if it stops
with an error), then: `godot --path game -- --agent-url=http://127.0.0.1:8787 --model --less-blood --mute --seed=123`
(everything after `--` is optional). The environment variable `BTC_AGENT_URL` also sets the
address. These have no script option:

| Game option | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| `--authored-order` | the visitors come in the order `rules.json` lists them (the tests and the capture script use it) |
| `--no-intro` | no opening cards, no first-night training and no counter guide opening by itself |
| `--no-training` | the opening cards, but no training: the counter guide opens by itself at the first patient instead, as before November 17 |
| `--no-surprises` | no snaps and no flares (the tests and the capture script use it and cue the ones they need) |
| `--lang=zh`, `--lang=en` | this language for the run, over the choice saved from the title card (the tests and the capture script use it; it is not saved) |

On November 15 the script was run once by an agent (offline, title screen, closed with the
window's close message): the game started and left cleanly. That was the build before the
changes of November 16 and 17. On November 18 the script started the November 17 build

reach the game.

## Languages

[Student, November 18: a language option on the title screen, Chinese first, with everything
that should be translated translated; and if one side is changed, the other must be changed
too.]

The title card has **English | 中文** in its top-right corner. A click switches every word the
game shows, and the choice is kept for the next start (`user://settings.cfg`). The language
changes only on the title card, where nothing is half-said.

- **What is translated.** Everything the player reads: the opening, the training, the guide,
  the sheet, the list, the intake card, every case's lines, the hints, the plates, the slip and
  the report. Words painted into pictures too: ten pictures have a Chinese twin,
  `<name>_zh.png` (the hall camera's label on the booth wall, the EXIT light, the shutter's
  stencil, the four things on the pinboards and the three imprints on the intake card), and
  the title sign reads 先别下刀 ("don't cut yet") over a small BEFORE THE CUT.
- **What stays English.** People's names and code names: Kip, Juno, the contacts' aliases, the
  model names on the sheet (KITE, VX-9), the key names. Companies, gangs and organizations are
  translated: Kessler is 凯斯勒, Harrow Credit 哈罗信贷, the Lanterns 灯笼帮, Trauma Team 创伤小组.
- **How.** Every word is written in English, in `data/` and in the scripts. The Chinese is a
  set of catalogs in `data/lang/zh/`, keyed by that exact English (`scripts/core/lang.gd`), and
  text is translated where it is drawn, so the rules, the data and the event log stay English.
  The Chinese glyphs are Fusion Pixel Font's 10-pixel cut (`assets/fonts/zh.fnt`, with its
  licence beside it), the fallback of every Counter Sans font: Latin letters and digits stay
  Counter Sans in both languages. Chinese is typed out and held at half the English rate per
  character, since a Chinese line says as much in about half the characters (`Lang.PER_CHAR`).
- **Typing.** While the typing line is open in Chinese, the game turns the system's input
  method on, places its candidate window at the caret and shows what is being composed there;
  the line takes hanzi and Chinese punctuation. Otherwise the input method is off, so the
  letter keys stay the game's keys.
- **The patients.** The model is asked to answer in the player's language (the proxy's
  `language` field), keeping names and code names in Latin letters, and in Chinese with the
  smaller limits of `agent.languages` in `rules.json` (55 characters a line, 110 a reply).
  Speech is transcribed in that language. The offline patient's lines are in the case catalogs,
  and it listens for Chinese words as well as English ones (`data/lang/zh/topics.json`).
- **The intake card in Chinese.** A hanzi is nine pixels tall where a capital is seven, so lines
  set nine pixels apart touch. In Chinese a row's one or two notes are set ten apart, and a
  finding puts 正常 or 异常 at the start of its text, with 你没有检查 on the right, instead of
  under it (the second line starts under the label). A row with three notes still sets them
  nine apart: they touch, but stay clear of the rule below.
- **Keeping the two in step.** A change to the English is a change to the Chinese, in the same
  commit. `python tools/check_lang.py` fails on a line with no translation, a translation whose
  English has changed, two translations of one line, a line and its translation with different
  `{slots}` or `%`-codes, a translation that is empty or the English again, and a picture
  without its twin. `measure_text.py --lang zh`
  measures every line as it reads in Chinese, and the scene test run with ` -- --lang=zh` fails
  on any English that reaches the screen untranslated. The glossary, the patients' voices and
  how a translation is added are in
  [chinese-translation.md](../docs/development/chinese-translation.md).

## The opening, the training and the guide

**The opening.** Once a session, after the title card, six short cards with small moving
pictures tell the story: FIRED (Kessler walks you out; you keep its spec binder), THE DEBT
(Kessler sells what you owe to Harrow Credit, which leases you your chair, your tools and your
own hands), THE LANTERNS (their back-street clinic, their contacts, tonight's list and what a
drop pays), THE LINE (240 an hour), THE DRIFT (street implants are illegal, cheap and sometimes
torn out of a soldier; some who wear them drift and can turn without warning, most are only
tired, scared or angry) and BEFORE THE CUT (check them against the binder; TREAT, TRAUMA TEAM,
the SHUTTER). Any click or ENTER moves on; ESC skips the rest.

[Student, November 18: the opening animation had hardly changed, though the story had.] The
pictures were redrawn for the story on November 18. FIRED is a strip of three panels: the badge
refused on Kessler's floor 88, the lift down between two guards, and outside in the rain, still
holding the binder, tabbed EYES, HANDS, PORTS and VOICE. THE DEBT is Kessler's staff debt file
stamped SOLD TO HARROW, with Harrow's lease slid over it. THE LANTERNS tags the matched patient
CONTACT and the parcel PACKAGE. THE DRIFT is a crate of military surplus with its serials filed
off and, through the glass, a visitor whose eye implant glitches before they crack the pane. No
picture shows one of the five visitors, and the list on the Lanterns card has two made-up
entries that fit none of them, so it cannot be taken for a real night's list.

**The first-night training.** [Student, November 17: the grip and the eye test are confusing;
the opening guidance should be clearer, ideally walking through everything once.] On the first
night of a session a small TRAINING box gives 14 tips, one at a time, each with a pulsing
frame round the one thing it is about: the contact list, how its clues work, the hall camera,
the intake card, saying a code, the drop, the reference sheet, the penlight, the grip, the
port, the talk pads with CALM's price, the marks, the decision and, on the first slip, a last
tip about snaps and flares. Every tip has **NEXT**, and **SKIP ALL [K]** ends the training.
Nothing blocks and nothing sets an order: a tip for something already done is passed over, a
tip that is only read gives way to the next action after 2 seconds, one that asks for
something after 10. The box keeps off the open papers, the slip, the big camera screen and the
patient's eyes, and steps out of the way while the guide or a close-up is open. It runs from
the end of the opening cards until the second visitor is buzzed in. On that night the first
visitor is always on the contact list, so the first visit can show how a code works, and that
visit has no snap or flare.

**The counter guide.** Four pages dim the screen and frame one part of the booth each (the
window, the left wall, the right wall, the desk), naming what each thing there is for. H, F1
or the COUNTER GUIDE booklet on the right wall opens it, between patients or during a visit
(only F1 while you are typing). It opens by itself at the first patient only when the training
is off (`--no-training`). While it is open, a paper that was down hangs back on its wall,
where the guide points at it, and comes down again when the guide closes.

Neither the cards, the training nor the guide is a step you have to take. Five hints appear
once each when they first matter: the first note on the card, the last patience lamp, the end
of the first attack, the first drop ("The drop changes nothing about the exam. Steady or
drifting, the call is still yours.") and the first flare ("Only temper: no red bar, nothing to
stop. The SHUTTER is for the red bar."). The words are in `data/intro.json`.

## The reference sheet

[Student, November 18: if every patient has the same normal sheet, it grows tiring; the
difference needs a reason in the story, such as different implant models. Organize it by
implant model rather than by character: a character is a set of parts, which is also easier to

The sheet is a page of the spec binder the doctor kept from Kessler: the factory normal of
every model Kessler built, licensed or saw copied on the street (`data/parts.json`). A patient
is a set of parts, one model in each slot: left eye, right eye, hands, port and voice (`parts`
in the case file). The sheet for the patient at the counter is put together from the models
they wear (`scripts/core/case_db.gd`, `sheet_for`), so two patients with the same models get the
same sheet, and a new patient needs only models from the binder. Its title names the patient
(REFERENCE SHEET: KIP MARLOW), and each row prints its model under the row's label in amber.
Between patients the sheet on the wall is the binder's baseline body, every part natural and a
street port, under the title NORMAL REFERENCE SHEET.

| Row | Normal, by model |
| --- | --- |
| EYES | NATURAL: "Pupils tighten in light. Both follow it." KITE (an optic) and IRIS (a display): no pupils, nothing tightens. TALON (a combat eye): shutters snap tight in light. Every model follows the light. Two different eyes print both halves, left first: "L: no pupil. R: tightens. Both follow.", tagged L KITE (or MIXED for two different implants) |
| HANDS | "Lets go on 'release' inside {limit} s.", with the model's limit: NATURAL 1.0, LOADER 1.5, SEAM 0.8, DEFT 0.5, REFLEX 0.3 |
| PORT | STREET and WORK: "Calm skin at the socket. No dark threads." MIL-SPEC: "Calm skin at the plate. No dark threads." |
| SPEECH | NATURAL and VX-9: "Says 'I' and 'my'. Remembers what they did." |
| TEMPER | the same for every body: "Pushed, face and voice react together." |

Each model also has its own line of what is ordinary and not a sign (a cracked lens for KITE,
locking up now and then for LOADER, a st-stutter for VX-9), so what looks odd but is harmless
changes with the body as well.

| Patient | Eyes, left / right | Hands | Port | Voice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kip | KITE / natural | natural | street | natural |
| Rook | natural | LOADER | work | natural |

Footer, the house rule: "Two broken lines = drifting: call TRAUMA TEAM." and "Strange is not
sick. A refusal is not a sign." The sheet's own words are `data/sheet.json`; the two is its
`threshold`. Every drifting case breaks at least that many lines (the data check refuses a case
that does not), and no steady case breaks any. When the data loads, `case_db.gd` also refuses a
case that wears a model the binder does not have, an eye that tightens with no pupil to
tighten, or a hands row whose verdict does not match its release time against its model's
limit. The rule is advice for the player: the game scores the call, never the marks.

**The sheet in the close-ups.** [Student, November 17: pass and fail are unclear; there is a
reference sheet, but nothing points at it, for example how long a grip is reasonable.] Every
tool's close-up holds the sheet up beside the picture: a **SHEET** line along its bottom gives
that tool's normal line, the patient's own. The penlight writes a readout under each eye once
it has had the light long enough: RIGHT EYE or LEFT EYE with the eye's model beside it in amber,
then what it did (TIGHTENS or STAYS WIDE for a natural pupil, NO PUPIL for a model with none,
SHUTTER SNAPS or SHUTTER OPEN for a combat shutter), then FOLLOWS or DOESN'T FOLLOW. The grip
timer runs on a scale of two seconds that starts at 0, with the limit of the patient's hands
model marked on it and labelled with it: **LIMIT 1s** for natural hands, LIMIT 1.5s for a
LOADER, LIMIT 0.3s for a REFLEX rig (`limit_ms` in `data/parts.json`; it is no longer in
`rules.json`). Past the mark the bar and the figure turn red. The readouts say what happened,
never steady or drifting: the sheet, the marks and the call stay the player's.

## Contacts and codes

[Student, November 17: the doctor, fired by a megacorp and in debt, works for a gang at a
street implant clinic. Contacts come in to exchange codes and smuggle military or high-end
implants, and illegally chromed cyberpsychos come in too. A daily contact list is used to
cross-reference the visitors; a wrong code costs patience, and each contact has a different
code.]

- **The list.** Each night two entries (`contacts.per_night`) are drawn from the pool in
  `data/contacts.json`, each for a different visitor who comes that night, with a generator
  seeded from the shift seed. The pool has two entries per visitor, ten in all, each with its
  own alias, clue pair, code line, counter-line and package. The list is not in arrival order.
- **The clues** use only what the player can see: the intake card (age, fee, request) and a
  look at the eyes (implants in both, or at least one natural eye). When the data loads,
  `scripts/core/case_db.gd` checks every clue against the five cases: each clue alone fits at
  least two visitors, and an entry's two clues together fit exactly one, its own. A data file
  that breaks this does not load. So the list never names anyone, and one clue alone never
  gives anyone away.
- **SAY.** During the exam each open entry on the list has a SAY button. The right contact
  answers with the entry's counter-line and hands a package over: the narrator says what it is,
  and 300 credits go into the till at once, whatever you decide. Anyone else, or a contact
  given the other entry's code, gives a blank look in their own words and loses 1 patience
  (`contacts.wrong_cost`). A code needs at least that much patience left, so a patient on the
  way out cannot be sounded for free. A right code costs nothing. A code a visitor has already
  heard cannot be said to them again, and after a drop the list is closed for the visit.
- **What a code is not.** It is not a test of the patient. A wrong code costs everybody the
  same, the blank look is in the visitor's own voice whatever their truth, and the contacts
  are steady and drifting alike. The drop changes neither the case, nor the call's credits,
  nor the chance of an attack.
- **On paper.** The list marks an entry PICKED UP after its drop, or MISSED when its contact
  came and went without one, and pencils in the codes a visitor did not know. The slip prints
  `DROP +300` or `ALIAS: NO DROP`; the report adds "Drops picked up: n of 2 (+X)" and, under each
  visit, `drop +300`, `drop missed` or `calm -N`.
- **Words.** On screen the people on the list are always contacts. Kip's own job is bike
  courier (his card says "Bike courier, 22"), so the gang's people are never called couriers
  where the player can read it; the code, the data notes and the tests still say courier for
  the role.

**The lender's line.** The meter wants 240 more in the till every hour (`line.per_hour`), so
hour n needs n x 240 and the night needs 1,200. After each slip the meter reads the till
against the line for that hour: over, or UNDER THE LINE in red. A reading under the line is
counted and shown in the report, but the shift goes on; only the last reading decides the
night. Closing under the line is the ending called THE CUT, whatever else went right.

Five right calls make 1,140: under the line. With one drop they make 1,440, with both 1,740,
less what CALM and glass cost. Treating everyone makes 100 and calling Trauma Team on everyone
makes -210. One needless call can be afforded only with both drops (1,240 when it is Juno, the
largest fee to lose); with one drop it cannot. Two needless calls end under the line, drops or
not (790 with both drops, for Rook and Juno). These totals are asserted by the rule tests, and
so is that every one of the 1,024 combinations of calls, with no drop and with two, ends in a
titled report, and that THE CUT is given exactly to the nights that end under the line. The
till can be spent on two things: CALM and new counter glass (below). All numbers are
first-pass values in `data/rules.json` that no player has tuned.

**When a patient turns** there is a warning line and a short beat. Then a red bar in the slot
of the sill fills from the warning to the moment they are through; its notches are the blows
on the glass. SPACE (or the SHUT lever) drops the shutter, and when you do it matters:

| The shutter comes down | The plate says | The glass |
| --- | --- | --- |
| before the first blow | STOPPED CLEAN: THE GLASS HOLDS | untouched |
| after one or two blows | STOPPED AFTER 1 BLOW / 2 BLOWS | one lamp out per blow |
| after the blow that takes the last lamp | STOPPED, BUT THE GLASS IS GONE | none left |
| in time, with no glass left to hit | STOPPED IN TIME: NO GLASS LEFT | none left |
| not in time | they come through | none left |

The pane takes three blows a night (`attack.glass`), and what it has taken stays until you pay
to have a layer put back or the shift ends. With whole glass the blows land 0.54, 0.99 and
1.44 seconds after the warning and the patient is through at 1.8. Less glass means fewer blows
before the break-through: 1.44 seconds with two lamps left, 0.99 with one, 0.65 with none. So
a sloppy stop on the first attacker makes the second one faster. A pane that has lost its last
lamp drops out of its frame behind the shutter, and the frame stays empty until you pay for a
layer; the next shift starts with a whole pane either way. Trauma Team is an authored outcome
with fixed lines; it is not another AI agent.

**Snaps and flares.** [Student, November 17: during the eye test, the grip or any other
action, the patient may suddenly fly into a rage and lunge, with transitions.]

- **Who.** Only a drifting patient snaps. Anyone may flare, at most once a visit. A snap that
  comes up wins over a flare. Neither happens on the training visit.
- **When.** Each action of the doctor's is rolled once when it has run its course: a tool, a
  question, pressure, CALM, a code, the doctor's own words. Looking is free and is not an
  action. A snap's chance is (actions - 3) x 0.08, never above 0.30: nothing for the first
  three actions, then 8, 16, 24 and 30 per cent. Computed from these numbers, a drifting
  patient snaps in about one exam in five of five actions, two in five of six and seven in ten
  of eight. A flare's chance is 0.10 for every action after the first.
- **How.** What came up waits 0.6 to 1.6 seconds (a flare 0.5 to 1.4), so it lands inside
  whatever the doctor is doing by then, though never in a reply on its way, a line still being
  typed, the typing line, the microphone or the guide. A close-up is no shelter: it is knocked
  out of the doctor's hands (it jolts, drops and fades). A snap is a surge at the glass with a
  bang, a shake and a red flash, and the attack starts 0.35 seconds later (`snap.lead_ms`)
  instead of after the usual beat; the red bar, the blows and the glass are those of any
  attack. Caught, a snap is a right call; missed, it is a hit (-300).
- **A flare** is a shout and a hand slammed on the glass, with a smaller surge and shake, but
  no red bar and nothing to stop. It is evidence for the TEMPER line only, and it agrees with
  the case: a steady patient's face and voice go together, a drifting patient's do not.
  Dropping the shutter on it is a false alarm (-100).
- Tying the snap to the number of actions gives it a rule a player can learn: a long exam of a
  drifting patient is dangerous, so knowing when to stop examining and decide is part of the
  game. The flare is there so that a shout at the glass is not proof by itself.

**Mending the glass.** [Student, November 16: also let the player pay to repair the counter's
blast glass when earlier visitors have damaged it.] Between patients, while the next one waits
in the hall, the GLASS key on the right wall (or G) orders one layer. It costs 40 credits
(`attack.repair_cost`; 0 switches repairs off) and puts one lamp back, up to the three of a
whole pane; an empty frame gets glass again. The key shows its price while a layer is missing
and can be ordered, and pointing at it says why when it cannot (a patient is at the counter,
or every layer is whole). The money comes off the till at once, and the till may go under zero
for it. The meter counts it at every later reading; readings already taken do not change. The
report shows what was paid ("Till, after 120 paid for glass"). A whole pane from an empty
frame costs 120, half an hour of the line at 240 an hour, so safety is bought with the same
credits the lender wants. The arm that fits the layer wipes the pane as it goes; blood on the
sill and the booth walls and the damage to the shutter stay until the shift ends. The price,
the debt and the absence of any other limit are first guesses that nobody has played against.

## Saying it in your own words

**Typing.** T (or ENTER on a quiet strip, or the TYPE button on the strip) opens a line of up
to 120 characters. ENTER says it, ESC takes it back. Offline, the patient only knows its
script: a sentence that matches one of its questions is answered as that question, anything
else gets an honest "offline" hint and costs nothing. In Chinese the offline patient listens
for Chinese words as well (Languages). With a patient AI service the sentence goes to the model
as the doctor's words and the patient answers for itself; the first such exchange costs 1
patience, the rest are free.

**Push-to-talk.** Offered only when a patient AI service is connected (`-AgentUrl ... -Model`).
Hold TAB, or hold the mouse on the SPEAK button, and talk; let go to stop. The title card says
which way of talking is on tonight ("Patient AI on: ... Hold TAB to speak, or press T to type."
or "Patient AI off: the authored patient answers. ..."), and offline TAB shows a hint that
says what speaking needs.

- The microphone is open only while the key is held. Nothing listens otherwise.
- While it is open the patient looks at you and their hands stop.
- Opening it takes about half a second on Windows (measured November 15). Until it is ready
  the strip says OPENING THE MICROPHONE...; speak when it says LISTENING. Letting go before
  that sends nothing and says so. The game keeps running while it opens.
- The strip shows a level meter and a clock while listening, then "writing it down".
- The words arrive **on the typing line**. Nothing is sent on key release: you read them,
  correct them if needed, then ENTER says them or ESC takes them back.
- A clip is 0.35 to 10 seconds, at most 24 per visit. It is kept in memory only, sent once to
  the proxy and dropped. Neither the clip nor the transcript is written to any log.
- A tap, silence, no microphone, no words, a slow or failed service and a full budget each
  show a short hint and leave whatever was typed. Typing and subtitles always work.
- ESC, right-click, losing window focus, a decision or the next patient cancel a clip. A
  transcript that arrives late for a cancelled clip or a previous patient is ignored.
- Game sound is turned down while the microphone is open.
- The patient does not speak aloud; replies are subtitles.

## Patient AI modes

- **Offline comparison** (default): authored lines and choices from the case files. No network.
- **Model** (`--agent-url=... --model`): every line and reaction is generated by the model
  behind the proxy from the character brief and the conversation so far. There is no answer
  list: the three questions under ASK are suggestions, and the answers to them are generated
  like the answer to anything you type or say. The model may also refuse a test. Instrument
  readings, evidence, attacks, snaps, flares, codes, scoring and Trauma Team stay with the
  game. The corner of the window shows `AI DIALOGUE`, `MOCK TRANSPORT` or `OFFLINE FALLBACK`
  with the calls used this visit (16 at most).
- **Codes are game-owned.** Saying a code makes no model call and adds nothing to the
  patient's conversation memory, so the patient AI can never confirm, deny or leak one.
- **The player's language** goes with every request. In Chinese the model is asked for
  Simplified Chinese with names and code names in Latin letters, within the smaller Chinese
  limits; the readings, the codes and the scoring do not change with the language.
- If a call fails, is late (4 s) or the budget is spent, the authored line is used and labelled.
- A quarter of a second after a question has gone to the service, the patient is seen thinking
  it over: each looks away in their own manner, with a small thought bubble by the head, until
  the answer comes. The pose follows the patient's habit, never whether they are steady or
  drifting. Offline the answer is there at once and no pose is shown.
- While a reply is on its way, or its first line is still typing, you can look, take a paper
  down or hang it back, mark the card, switch the hall screen on, pick up the TREAT stamp, open
  the Trauma Team line and use the shutter. A look shows its own line and keeps the dots away
  for 1.6 s. TREAT or the call decide at once and drop the reply (`reply_dropped` in the event
  log). Asking, tools, CALM, PRESS, codes, typing and speaking wait; after 0.8 s the strip says
  "Still answering. You can look meanwhile, or decide now."

The proxy, its limits and what a live setup needs are in `../agent_proxy/README.md`.

After changing art: `python build_all.py` in `tools/art`, then
`& $GODOT --headless --path game --import`. The `.import` and `.uid` files are part of the
repository; commit them with the assets. (Godot marks the font atlas pictures `skip`: the
`.fnt` files load them.) A picture with words in it is drawn twice, `<name>.png` and
`<name>_zh.png`; change its words in `tools/art/words.py`, never in one picture alone.

## Known limits

- The only live run is the one of November 15 (30 patient replies, ten transcriptions; see the
  live report linked at the top). It was made before the changes of November 16, 17 and 18,
  and no paid service has been called since. The thinking pose, the leaving countdown, the
  hourly line, the counter-first screen, the codes, the flares and the snaps have therefore
  never run against a live model, and one run says little about believability. No model has
  been asked to answer in Chinese, and no Chinese speech has been transcribed: whether the
  patients sound right in Chinese, keep to its lengths and keep names in Latin letters is
  untested.
- The Chinese was written by an assistant and has not been read by a person: not the
  translation, not its fit on screen in play, and not the patients' voices in it
  ([glossary and voices](../docs/development/chinese-translation.md)). Typing Chinese has been
  checked only by the scene test, which hands the typing line what an input method would (the
  characters, and the text still being composed); no one has typed with a real input method in
  the game window, so where its candidate list appears and how it behaves is unknown.
- In Chinese, a row of the intake card with three notes sets them nine pixels apart, so they
  touch. A Chinese line may break inside a word (常 | 见), which Chinese allows but a careful
  typesetter would avoid.
- The counter-first screen was designed by an assistant from a two-line request of the
  student; the student's notes of November 17 are about it, and the answers to them have not
  been reviewed. What hangs where, the one hook shared by the sheet and the list, the keys R,
  L, N, C, G and K, the card that comes down by itself for the stamp and the findings, and the
  transcript unit in the desk are all proposals. A paper that is down covers the top of one
  side of the window (132 of its 440 pixels in width); whether that is comfortable in play is
  unknown.
- Every number added on November 16, 17 and 18 is a first guess that nobody has played against:
  240 an hour for the line, 300 a drop, two contacts a night, 1 patience for a wrong code, 30
  credits for CALM, a snap's 3 free actions, 0.08 a step and 0.30 at most, a flare's 0.10, the
  0.35 s between a snap and its attack, eight seconds before a patient with no patience leaves,
  an attack window of 1.8 s with blows at 0.54, 0.99 and 1.44 s, three glass lamps a night,
  0.65 s with none left, 40 credits for a layer, two crosses for the house rule, and the hands
  limits of the binder (1.5 s for a LOADER, 0.8 for SEAM, 0.5 for DEFT, 0.3 for REFLEX). The tests
  press the shutter within one frame, so nobody knows yet whether a person can make those
  windows, a snap's above all, or whether they are too easy.
- Whether the training says too much or too little, whether the clue pairs are fun to
  cross-reference or a chore, whether anyone still buys CALM, and whether a flare is read as
  temper or panics the player into a false alarm are questions for play.
- The night cannot be lost before closing: an hour under the line costs nothing by itself, and
  only the till at the end of the fifth hour decides. The glass is whole again every shift.
- The story (Kessler, Harrow Credit, the Lanterns), the opening cards and their pictures, the
  spec binder and its models, the training tips, the guide's wording, the coach hints, the
  hints on the wall objects, the fitter's line, the contacts' aliases, clues, codes and
  packages, and the flare lines were written by an assistant and have not been reviewed by the
  student.
- The marks on the intake card are the player's own notes. After the visit they are shown
  beside what was true; they are never scored, and no rule reads them.
- While the patient is answering (up to 4 s with a slow service), asking, tools, CALM, PRESS,
  codes, typing and speaking wait; looking, the papers, marking, the stamp, the call line and
  the shutter do not (above).
- The typing line has no paste. A transcript keeps only what the two fonts can draw (Latin
  letters, and Chinese from the fallback); one with nothing left is refused with a hint.
- A typed provocation has no rule effect of its own: only the PRESS pad moves patience and
  can set a patient off. A typed code is not a code either: only SAY on the list says one.
  Exit, attack, leaving, code and Trauma Team lines are authored in both modes.
- The same five patients come every shift, with the same truths and the same signs; only the
  order, the two contacts and their entries change with the seed (and the first shift of a
  session opens with a steady patient). Signs are not recombined at random, and patients do not
  draw new models. No saved game, no settings screen (the language choice is the one thing
  kept between runs), no export build.
- `--less-blood` was judged from the frames captured with it and from five picture checks and
  three blood counts in the scene test. Of the final drawing's 91 frames an assistant looked at
  16, as contact sheets (the strikes, the blood moments, the aftermaths, the stop plates, the
  snap and the fitter), and enlarged three of them (Maren's strike and blood moments and Vale's
  blood moment) beside the same moments drawn with blood. They show no splatter, drips, pools or
  blood on the hands. What stays red is the tint and the edge of the screen, Vale's optics and
  the pin on Vale's coat (there when Vale walks in), and the dark round mark on Maren's cheek is
  the after-image of the cutting torch's flash, drawn the same with blood. Blood that only shows
  between the captured moments would have been missed.
- Closing the window stops every sound and leaves 0.2 seconds later, which ends Godot's
  `4 ObjectDB instances were leaked at exit` warning. That is checked headlessly with the dummy
  audio driver only. A run that ends the engine another way while the ambience plays, such as
  `--quit-after` (the boot checks above), still prints the warning.
- Nobody has operated the build of November 18 in a visible window: it has run only headless
  and on a hidden desktop, driven by scripts. The build of November 17 was opened in a visible
  window for the student on November 18, who looked at its opening; there is no record of more
  play. The build of November 15 was operated by an agent, through posted window messages. The sound has been measured, not listened to. No
  sound file was added on November 17: the snap, the flare, the list, the codes and the
  training reuse existing sounds, and how they sit in the mix has not been measured. Of
  November 16, `glass_fit.wav` (the arm that fits new glass) is generated and played when a
  layer is ordered, but the tests run with the dummy audio driver, its place in the mix has not
  been measured and nobody has heard it. On November 15 the attack sounds were turned down 9 dB
  (`ATTACK_TRIM_DB` in `main.gd`): measured from a silent recording of the mix, the loudest
  second of an attack went from about 24 dB over the rain to 19 dB (caught) and 22 dB
  (landed), and the limiter from 21% / 59% of the time to 0% / 8%. The whine now fades in
  0.12 s when the shutter drops early, and the file ends with a 60 ms release instead of a
  cut. Whether that is loud enough, or too loud, needs a person with headphones.
- Blood from an attack stays until the shift ends. So does broken glass, unless you pay for
  new layers.
- No play session by a person is recorded for this version.
